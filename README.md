# Crop yield prediction

This project estimates yield in kg/ha from entered weather, soil, application, crop, season, and state values. The web form accepts temperature, humidity, light, soil moisture, pH, rainfall, fertilizer, and pesticide. The trained historical model uses only features that exist in its source data; soil moisture and light are accepted for the planned ESP32 interface but are not used in prediction because the training data has no paired measurements for them. The farmer is not asked for N/P/K.

Weather inputs may be populated online after selecting a state and location. Defaults use the mean of the last five complete calendar years of annual district weather: mean of daily temperature and relative-humidity averages per year, and yearly precipitation sums averaged across years. This matches the source CSV's district/year weather semantics. Current Open-Meteo temperature and humidity are shown as reference only. Open-Meteo Archive is primary; NASA POWER Daily is the historical fallback. Use the per-field reset action to restore the latest fetched default; manual edits remain the values submitted to prediction.

`data/india_locations.csv` supplies bundled common district/city coordinates. Other districts in the existing selector are resolved on demand through Open-Meteo geocoding and cached. Historical requests are cached for 24 hours, current conditions for 30 minutes, each HTTP attempt times out after 10 seconds with two retries, and API failures leave manual entry available. The 30-day summary and current readings are informational only; the model receives the annual defaults or whatever values the user edits in the three form fields. Weather edits outside temperature −10–55°C, humidity 0–100%, or rainfall 0–12,000 mm/year show a warning and are outside the accepted prediction range.

Run the weather-service tests with `python -m unittest -v test_weather_service.py`. The existing model checks remain `python sanity_tests.py`.

## Train and use

```powershell
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python train.py
python sanity_tests.py
python predict.py --crop Wheat --state Punjab --season Rabi --soil-type Loamy --temperature 26 --rainfall 900 --humidity 60 --ph 6.8 --fertilizer 120 --pesticide 0.2
python app.py
```

Training writes `models/yield_model.joblib`, `models/training_report.json`, and the auditable `models/synthetic_stress_rows.csv`. Prediction returns a point estimate, a 90% absolute-residual interval, and each stress factor. `sanity_tests.py` prints and plots rainfall, fertilizer, and temperature sensitivity to `sensitivity_curves.svg`; it exits with an error if its failure/ideal/monotonic-response assertions fail.

## Model design

The supervised component is a histogram gradient boosting regressor trained on `log1p(Yield)` with monotonic constraints for rainfall and fertilizer (increasing) and pesticide (decreasing). A transparent multiplier applies input-only stress factors for water, temperature, fertilizer, soil pH, soil class, and pesticide, then clips only to `[0, 60,000] kg/ha`. This lets predictions approach zero below the observed training minimum. It does not clamp to the training minimum. The safety ceiling is an engineering bound informed by the dataset's scale, not a verified biological maximum for every crop.

Five-fold `GroupKFold` validation holds out complete district-region groups. Synthetic stress rows are generated within each training fold only and excluded from validation scoring. They are flagged `is_synthetic` in the training frame and generated by reducing rainfall to 5–25% of its paired observed value, raising temperature 5–12°C, setting fertilizer/pesticide to zero, and setting pH to 4.0 or 9.5. Their labels are the paired observed yield multiplied by input-derived water, heat, fertilizer, and pH factors. These are counterfactual examples, not observed crop failures, and are disclosed separately in the report. The script compares ridge and boosted log-target pipelines and reports RMSE, MAE, R², and MAE on the lowest/highest yield deciles.

Stress-function assumptions are intentionally visible in `train.py`: water rises linearly to a crop-specific 75th-percentile rainfall threshold, saturates, then declines above a flood proxy; temperature declines beyond a crop-specific median band; fertilizer rises to its crop-specific 75th percentile and saturates; pH departs from its crop-specific median; pesticide only incurs a small penalty above its historical 90th percentile. Temperature supplied to the learned estimator is limited to the crop's observed 1st–99th percentile range, while the stress function still uses the actual entered temperature, which avoids unsupported tree extrapolation and cliff-like outputs. A coarse soil-class factor is an explicit prior (Mollisols 1.00, Alfisols .94, Inceptisols .90, Vertisols .86, Entisols .82, Aridisols .72) and is not a field-specific soil fertility measurement. Thresholds are computed from inputs, never yield. They are guardrails for this prototype, not a calibrated crop growth simulator.

## Feature and data limits

The available matched dataset has 198,369 records from 1966–2017. Yield-only records through 2024/25 do not contain matching weather/sensor inputs and are not used to train this input-driven model. `Year`, district/region identifier, row IDs, N/P/K (not reliably farmer-measured here), yield-derived location aggregates, and target encodings are excluded from model inputs. State is parsed from the dataset's region string; district is used only to group cross-validation and populate location/soil labels. `Soil Type` is a physical source attribute, and app selects its typical label from the chosen location. Soil pH in the source is district-level/proxy-like, not a field measurement. Rainfall is annual district rainfall, not crop-season effective rainfall. The app's sensor moisture/light readings are not trained features.

Historical correlations and feature ranges are printed on each training run. A very low annual rainfall input is forced to reduce yield, but exact agronomic response is uncertain because the dataset lacks paired field-level irrigation, soil moisture, planting date, cultivar, and crop-stage observations. Synthetic data improves behavior under severe inputs but cannot replace real measured crop failures. The conformal interval is based on absolute errors from held-out-region predictions; under distribution shift it may not have 90% coverage. Model predictions are decision support, not a guaranteed farm outcome.

## Saved field reports

Submitting the existing field form creates a shareable `/report/<id>` page and stores the inputs, deterministic analysis, and narrative in `data/reports.sqlite3`. The report can be refreshed or reopened from its URL. The Back link returns to the form; the draft is preserved in the browser session. Download PDF produces a ReportLab PDF with vector charts. What-if preview recomputes results when fertilizer, pesticide, soil pH, annual temperature, humidity, or rainfall changes. Fertilizer, pesticide, and pH curves are model previews, not chemical dose recommendations. Pesticide decisions require a confirmed pest/disease and the product label. Soil pH correction must be based on a soil test; the app never invents lime, sulfur, or gypsum rates. Irrigation, sowing date, and variety are unavailable because they were not model features.

Risk score is a documented screening score from 0 to 100: up to 40 points for a normal approximation of probability below half the regional average using the model interval width; up to 25 for annual rainfall deficit/excess versus historical location records; up to 20 for temperature outside the crop/season 10th–90th percentile band; up to 10 for a high-humidity disease screen; and up to 15 for out-of-range inputs. Status bands are Low (0–24), Moderate (25–49), High (50–74), and Severe (75–100). This is a decision-support heuristic, not a calibrated probability of crop loss. Local feature contributions are model perturbations from crop/season input medians, not causal estimates or SHAP values.

Gemini AI summary is optional and server-side. Copy `.env.example` to `.env`, set `GEMINI_API_KEY` (and optionally `GEMINI_MODEL`), then restart Flask. No key is stored in code or sent to the browser. The backend sends structured report inputs, crop, season, location, estimate, and risk details to Gemini; it validates the JSON response and removes sentences with unsupported numbers. Anthropic remains an optional compatibility fallback when Gemini is not configured. If no provider key is configured, the report uses the built-in farmer-friendly summary. Regenerating advice creates a new report and requests a fresh Gemini summary. The what-if preview itself never calls AI.

Report checks: `python -m unittest -v test_reports.py test_weather_service.py`; existing UI mock: `node test_weather_ui.js`; model sanity checks: `python sanity_tests.py`.
